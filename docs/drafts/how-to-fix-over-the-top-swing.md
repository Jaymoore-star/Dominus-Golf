## The answer, first

*(This is the passage Google and AI answers are most likely to quote. Keep it
self-contained: 40-60 words.)*

An over the top swing starts the downswing with the shoulders and arms
instead of the lower body, so the club moves outside the target line and cuts
across the ball. That causes pulls and slices. The fix is to start down with
the lower body and let the trail elbow drop to the side, so the club arrives
from the inside.

## What "over the top" means

At the top of the backswing, the club should drop slightly onto a path from
inside the target line. In an over the top swing, the upper body unwinds
first and throws the club outward. The club then swings across the ball from
out to in.

**How to tell if you do it:**
- Your misses are pulls (straight left for a right-hander) or slices that
  start left and curve right.
- Your divots point left of the target.
- On a down-the-line video, the club head moves outside your hands early in
  the downswing.

## Why it happens

`[JEET: reorder by what you see most]`

1. **The shoulders start the downswing.** The most common cause. The
   shoulders spin open first, and the arms and club are thrown outward.
2. **The lower body doesn't lead.** If pressure stays on the trail foot,
   nothing pulls the club down on the inside, so the upper body takes over.
3. **The arms come away from the body.** When the trail elbow flies away from
   the side, the club has no route to the inside.
4. **The setup builds it in.** Shoulders open at address, the ball too far
   forward, or a weak grip all make an outside path the easy one.

## 5 drills to fix an over the top swing

*(Each drill uses the same blocks: what it fixes, how to do it, reps, what you
should feel, and the common mistake.)*

### Drill 1: The headcover drill
**Fixes:** the out-to-in path itself, by giving instant feedback.

1. Set up to a ball as normal.
2. Place a headcover just outside the ball line, about 6-12 inches behind the
   ball (toward your trail foot). `[JEET: confirm distances]`
3. Make half swings, then full swings, without touching the headcover.

**Reps:** 3 sets of 10. **You should feel:** the club swinging *out* toward
the target rather than across it. **Common mistake:** steering with the hands
to dodge the headcover. The change has to come from the sequence (Drills 2-4).

`[PHOTO 1: headcover placement, down-the-line view]`

### Drill 2: The pump drill
**Fixes:** the trail elbow and the first move down.

1. Swing to the top and stop.
2. Start down until your hands reach hip height, feeling the trail elbow drop
   toward your trail hip with the hands staying in front of your chest.
3. Go back to the top. Repeat twice, then swing through on the third pump.

**Reps:** 10 balls. **You should feel:** the club "drop" behind you before it
goes forward. **Common mistake:** pumping with the arms only while the
shoulders still open early.

`[PHOTO 2: hip-height checkpoint, trail elbow close to the body]`

### Drill 3: The step drill
**Fixes:** the lower body not leading.

1. Address the ball with your feet together.
2. As the backswing finishes, step your lead foot toward the target.
3. Let that step start the downswing, then swing through.

**Reps:** 10 balls. **You should feel:** the downswing starting from the
ground up, with the arms arriving last. **Common mistake:** stepping and
swinging at the same moment. The step comes first.

`[PHOTO 3: mid-step, lead foot planting, club still at the top]`

### Drill 4: The Tour Pure 9-to-3 drill
**Fixes:** sequence and impact position, with built-in feedback.

This is the Dominus method from the [Tour Pure guide](/tour-pure-guide).
Train impact first, then build the swing around it.

1. Swing the Tour Pure to the 9 o'clock position (P3): lead arm parallel to
   the ground, wrists hinged, chest turned away from the target.
2. Start down by rotating the trail ankle toward the target (P6). The hips
   slide toward the target, and the hands and elbow slide *inside, not over
   the top*.
3. Deliver to impact (P7) and check the logo: if you can clearly see the
   Dominus Golf logo on the trainer, the structure held. If not, you
   released early.
4. Swing through to 3 o'clock.

**Reps:** 100 a day, slowly. Speed is not the goal. **Safety:** use outdoors
or in a training facility with at least 10 feet of clear space. See the
[safety guidance](/safety-disclaimer).

### Drill 5: The connection drill
**Fixes:** the arms coming away from the body.

Wear the [Feel Right Band](/product/feel-right-band) and make half swings
with a real club, keeping the reference points in contact through the
takeaway and the start of the downswing. `[JEET: describe the band's three
contact points in one line each - the band guide explains them]`

**Reps:** 20 half swings, then 10 full. **You should feel:** the arms and
body turning together. **Common mistake:** squeezing so hard the swing gets
stiff. Keep contact, not tension.

## A two-week practice plan

| Days | Session (about 20 minutes) |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Drill 2 (pump) and Drill 3 (step), no target. Learn the sequence. |
| 4-7 | Add Drill 1 (headcover) at half speed. |
| 8-10 | Drill 4 (Tour Pure 9-to-3), 100 slow reps a day. |
| 11-14 | Full swings with the headcover in place. Check divots and ball flight. |
